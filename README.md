### Disease: SCN9A Congenital Insensitivity to Pain
**Background:** 

Channelopathy-associated Congenital Insensitivity to Pain (CIP) is an exceptionally rare genetic disorder characterized by a complete, lifelong inability to perceive physical pain from birth. Affected individuals cannot feel noxious stimuli resulting from severe trauma, deep burns, bone fractures, or internal inflammatory conditions such as appendicitis. Additionally, most patients present with anosmia (a loss of the sense of smell) because the underlying channel defect also affects olfactory neurons. Due to the lack of protective pain sensation, young children frequently experience unintentional self-inflicted injuries, including severe biting of the tongue, lips, and cheeks during teething. Over time, undetected micro-trauma often leads to serious orthopedic complications such as joint destruction (Charcot joints), chronic osteomyelitis, and poorly healed fractures. Despite these severe sensory deficits, tactile perception—including touch, pressure, vibration, and temperature differentiation—remains functional, and individuals typically demonstrate normal cognitive development, motor control, and autonomic function (such as normal sweating and heart rate regulation).
